FAQ

Fair questions.

Will it ever actually cancel something?

It's built so it can't: the “confirm cancellation” button isn't an action it can take. Every screen is checked before any click, and on anything unclear it backs out. Rare services cancel with no confirmation step at all; we track those and skip them.

What's a “leaving price”?

The offer a company makes when you go to cancel — a discount or free months to keep you. Most people never see it because they never start cancelling. Walkaway starts, gets it, and stops.

So what does it actually do?

The same thing a savvy person does by hand: go to cancel, sit through the “are you sure?” screens, and take the offer the company makes to keep you. Then it stops. Your plan stays exactly as it was — just cheaper.

Which services work?

Any that make an offer when you go to leave — streaming, news, music, software, VPNs, telecom, fitness apps. Sometimes a company won't make one; we leave those alone.

What if there's no discount?

You pay nothing. Your card is saved at checkout but never charged.

Is this a one-time thing?

Mostly. Offers run for a set term — months to a year — and most services won't offer again for a while. Run Walkaway again when an offer ends.

How is the fee calculated?

10% of the verified savings over each offer's term, added up, charged once, $1 minimum. Example: $24 → $7 a month for six months is $102 saved, so the fee is $10.20. If some of the services you picked don't come through, the charge is lower — you only ever pay on what was verified.

What does the extension look at?

Which sites you're signed into — it sends only those site names to identify subscription services, then reads your plan from each account page. During a run it only touches the tab it's working in. Cookies, passwords, and your browsing history never leave your browser.
