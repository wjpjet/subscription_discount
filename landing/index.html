The trick, explained

Companies show their best price to the people about to leave.

Retention offers exist because it costs far more to win you back than to keep you. So the real price hides behind the “Cancel” button. Walkaway goes and gets it.

Account settings→ Cancel subscription→ “Why are you leaving?”→ “Wait — 60% off for 6 months?”

Confirm cancellationThis button isn't in our toolset. Walkaway can't press it — not “won't,” can't. If there's no offer, it backs out.

Every step is classified before we act: is this an offer, or is this the point of no return? On anything ambiguous, Walkaway retreats. The only two outcomes are “you're paying less” or “nothing changed.”

FAQ

Fair questions.

Will it ever actually cancel something?

It's designed so it can't. The “confirm cancellation” button isn't an action it can take — it's not in its toolset at all. Every screen is classified before any click, and on anything ambiguous it backs out.

The honest caveat: a small number of services finalize a cancellation with no offer and no confirmation step. We track which ones do this and skip them entirely rather than attempt them.

Which services does it work with?

Ones that make loyalty offers when you go to leave — streaming, news, audio, software, telecom are common. Some services (Netflix, for example) never make offers; we know which, and we leave those alone rather than waste a run. Coverage grows as we add services.

What if there's no discount?

You pay nothing. The fee only exists when we've verified a lower price on your billing page.

How is the fee calculated?

10% of the verified savings over the term of the offer, with a $1 minimum. Example: a service drops you from $24 to $7 a month for six months — that's $102 saved, so the fee is $10.20. Charged once, after we've confirmed the new price.

Do you store my passwords?

No. Walkaway runs as a browser extension in your own browser, using the sessions you're already logged into. Passwords never leave your machine because we never touch them.

What does the extension actually look at?

To find your subscriptions, it checks — locally, in your browser — which of our known subscription services you're currently signed into, then reads your plan and price from each account page. During a hunt, it only touches the tab of the service it's working on. Nothing about your browsing is uploaded.

Do I have to sit and watch it?

No. It works in a background tab while you keep doing whatever you were doing. Open the side panel to follow along, or bring the tab to the front any time to watch it click through.

Does it run while my computer is off?

Not today. It runs in your browser while it's open — you can watch it go. Scheduled re-hunts when offers expire are on the roadmap.

Is this allowed?

You're using your own accounts, in your own browser, on your own click — Walkaway just does the tedious part. Some services' terms discourage automation, so we act only when you tell us to, and we back off rather than push through anything a service blocks.
